<h1>Teaching &amp; Curriculum Development</h1>
<nav class="page-toc" aria-label="On this page"><strong>On this page</strong><ul><li><a href="#curriculum">Curriculum Development</a></li><li><a href="#teaching-areas">Teaching Areas</a></li><li><a href="#philosophy">Teaching Philosophy</a></li><li><a href="#mentorship">Student Mentorship</a></li></ul></nav>
<p class="lead">My teaching helps students understand how information, technology, and analytical methods shape organizations, business processes, and decision-making.</p>
<p>As an Information Systems and Business Analytics educator, I emphasize conceptual foundations, analytical reasoning, and applied problem-solving. Students should understand not only how methods and technologies work, but also when they are appropriate, what assumptions they involve, and how to evaluate their limitations.</p>
<h2 id="curriculum">Curriculum Development</h2>
<p>At the University of Wisconsin–Eau Claire, I initiated and developed two undergraduate courses contributing to the College of Business curriculum. Both connect enduring disciplinary foundations with emerging business and technological challenges.</p>
<div class="pub"><h3>IS 480 — Advanced Business Analytics</h3><p>I developed and have taught this upper-division course, which introduces predictive and exploratory modeling, machine learning, text analytics, and the evaluation of analytical results. Students practice formulating business questions, selecting suitable methods, assessing data and model limitations, and communicating defensible findings. The course contributes to the undergraduate business curriculum and Business Analytics certificate.</p></div>
<div class="pub"><h3>IS 360 — AI Systems in Business</h3><p>I initiated and developed this undergraduate course to build students' understanding of AI systems, their capabilities and limitations, and their implications for business processes and organizational decision-making. The course emphasizes foundational understanding and responsible evaluation of AI in organizational contexts. It has received curriculum approval and is scheduled for its first offering in Spring 2027.</p></div>
<h2 id="teaching-areas">Teaching Areas</h2>
<h3>Information Systems and Business Processes</h3>
<p>How information systems support organizational operations, processes, and decisions, including relationships among people, information, technology, and business needs.</p>
<h3>Business Analytics and Data-Driven Decision-Making</h3>
<p>How to formulate analytical questions, assess evidence, evaluate methods, and interpret results in a business context.</p>
<h3>Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies</h3>
<p>Foundations, applications, opportunities, and limitations of AI and emerging technologies, with attention to organizational implications and responsible use.</p>
<h3>Systems Analysis and Development</h3>
<p>How information systems are conceptualized, designed, and developed to address organizational problems and support business objectives.</p>
<h2 id="philosophy">Teaching Philosophy</h2>
<p>Technical proficiency alone is not enough for effective decision-making. I emphasize three complementary learning goals: <strong>conceptual understanding</strong> of systems and analytical foundations; <strong>applied problem-solving</strong> through business-relevant exercises and projects; and <strong>critical evaluation</strong> of information quality, methodological choices, and technological limitations.</p>
<p>My research on digital behavior, information quality, trust, and human–AI interaction informs this approach, reinforcing the importance of independent judgment when interpreting information and AI-assisted outputs.</p>
<h2 id="mentorship">Student Mentorship and Engagement</h2>
<p>I serve as the faculty advisor and mentor for the <a href="https://blugoldconnect.uwec.edu/leadershiptech/home/">Leadership &amp; Technology Club</a> at UW–Eau Claire. This role complements my classroom teaching through student mentorship and engagement beyond formal coursework.</p>
